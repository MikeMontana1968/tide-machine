Arm Station Study

Two neighbouring stations, O1 and M2, at the 56 mm pitch. This is the tightest pair, because M2's arm sweeps over O1's flag disc. Each rotor is two printed parts: a hub with a flag disc, and a bolt-on arm carrying a V623ZZ, the same V-groove bearing as the rail pulleys, on a short M3 axle. A Vishay TCST2202 sits flat on the faceplate at 12 o'clock, and a fin on the back of the disc passes through its beam once per turn. Above each shaft, two V-groove bearings share one axle. The line drops from one, U-turns under the arm's bearing and climbs to the other, and the threading is mirrored at alternate stations. Press Play to watch everything clear everything else.

Threading

Each station's line comes in over one pulley, drops on the +x side, U-turns under the arm's bearing, and climbs on the −x side to the other pulley. Alternate stations swap the order, so every run between stations stays in one plane. Fleet angle is the worst skew of a leg out of its pulley's plane (±2.25 mm over the shortest leg). It peaks only at the top of M2's stroke. Because the arm and rail bearings are the same part, the legs hang exactly vertical at mid-stroke, whatever the real groove radius turns out to be.

Per station

Printed: hub + flag disc (print disc-down, so the hub and fin grow up), arm (print flat, boss up), sensor pad, pulley bracket.
Bought: 28BYJ-48, TCST2202 + 180 Ω + 10 kΩ, 3 × V623ZZ (2 on the rail, on one M3×20 + nyloc with a 3×5×0.5 shim; 1 on the arm, on an M3×8 + washer into an M3×4 heat-set insert), 2 × M3 + nut (sensor), 2 × M3 + nut (motor), 2 × M2 (arm to disc), 1 × M3 grub screw, 2 × #4 wood screw.

Why two printed rotor parts. The fin has to point back toward the plate, and the bearing boss forward. No single part prints both without support. Splitting them also means that when real NOAA constituents replace the placeholders, only the arms get reprinted. The hubs, flag discs and sensors stay put.

Why 12 o'clock. The TCST2202's leads leave through its seating face, so they pass through the faceplate. At 12 o'clock, space behind the plate is clear: the 28BYJ-48 hangs below its shaft, and its body reaches only 6 mm above the shaft centre. At 6 o'clock the leads would run into the motor's wire cover. The line runs in front of the arm, so it never reaches the sensor.

Resolution. The TCST2202's 0.5 mm aperture switches over 0.4 mm of shutter travel. At the fin's radius that is about 1.1° for the full 10–90% swing; the edge itself repeats far better than that. The machine only turns one way, so the timing edge is always the same edge.

Built by station_study.py, which also prints these clearances. Sensor dimensions are from Vishay document 81147; motor dimensions from the Kiatronics STEPD-01 datasheet. The line is drawn from the pulley tangent points, a close approximation. Arms turn at constant speed here; the firmware inverse makes their real speed uneven.
