Form factor F = (K1 + O1) / (M2 + S2) is the standard way to classify a coast: below 0.25 semidiurnal, 0.25–1.5 mixed mainly semidiurnal, 1.5–3.0 mixed mainly diurnal, above 3.0 diurnal. Watch it move as you drag the diurnal sliders — the character of a coastline really is just an amplitude ratio.

The epicycle view chains each constituent tip-to-tail as a rotating vector: length = amplitude, angle = σt − g. The horizontal reach of the chain's tip is the water level. This is why gear trains suit tides — the constituent frequencies come out of an epicyclic decomposition of the Earth–Moon–Sun geometry in the first place.

Angular speeds σ are the standard Doodson values in degrees per mean solar hour and are fixed. The amplitudes and phase lags in the presets are illustrative of a coastal type, not a surveyed station — take real ones from NOAA CO-OPS or the UKHO. Astronomical prediction only: no surge, wind or barometric pressure.
