Tide machine · Rev M study

Arm stations

Each station is an arm on its motor shaft carrying a V-groove bearing (V623ZZ) on a short M3 axle. Above each shaft, two V-groove pulleys share one axle. The line comes in over one, drops to the arm's bearing, U-turns under it, and climbs to the other. The threading is mirrored at alternate stations. The bearing swings sideways as well as up and down, which bends the output away from a sine. The firmware inverse turns the arm at an uneven rate so that the line length comes out exactly sinusoidal. The model takes its five constituents (M2, S2, N2, K1, O1) from the sliders above at 35 mm per metre, and scores them against every constituent switched on above.

Form factor F = (K1 + O1) / (M2 + S2) is the standard way to classify a coast: below 0.25 semidiurnal, 0.25–1.5 mixed mainly semidiurnal, 1.5–3.0 mixed mainly diurnal, above 3.0 diurnal. Watch it move as you drag the diurnal sliders — the character of a coastline really is just an amplitude ratio.

The epicycle view chains each constituent tip-to-tail as a rotating vector: length = amplitude, angle = σt − g. The horizontal reach of the chain's tip is the water level. This is why gear trains suit tides — the constituent frequencies come out of an epicyclic decomposition of the Earth–Moon–Sun geometry in the first place.

Angular speeds σ are the standard Doodson values in degrees per mean solar hour and are fixed. The amplitudes and phase lags in the presets are illustrative of a coastal type, not a surveyed station — take real ones from NOAA CO-OPS or the UKHO. Astronomical prediction only: no surge, wind or barometric pressure.

Arm stations model the Rev M proposal. Line length is computed exactly: legs tangent to the sleeve groove, rise to the coaxial V-groove pulley pair above the station: legs at ±4.8 mm (the pulley root radius), 65.2 mm above the shaft (rails 152 mm apart). The mirrored threading swaps which leg is which, and the peak torques are the same either way. The ±2.25 mm between the two pulley planes is ignored here; it changes take-up by under 0.5%, and the firmware table includes it. Friction uses the capstan law, eμθ, at every eye and sleeve, with the sliding direction worked out from how fast each station is taking up line. The pen hangs on a 180° pulley on a 41 g carriage, so the line runs at 0.20 N. The arm carries the same V623ZZ as the rail pulleys (groove root 4.8 mm), so its legs hang vertical at mid-stroke. V-groove bearings use an effective μ of 0.005. The superseded alternative, a PLA sleeve on an Ø8 printed pin, is kept as a switch: oiled PLA on PLA (μ 0.10) acting at the pin's 4 mm radius.
