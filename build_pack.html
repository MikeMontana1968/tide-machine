Tide machine · Rev M

Build Pack

Everything to order and how it wires together, for the Rev M design: five arm stations and the drum on 28BYJ-48 steppers, a BKA30D-R5 turning the calendar dial, seven Hall latches reading magnet pairs through the aluminium faceplate, and 22 V623ZZ bearings carrying the line. One through-hole main board behind the faceplate carries all the electronics; everything else plugs into it. Quantities to order include spares.

Wiring

Everything inside the dashed outline is on the main board; the rest plugs into its edge sockets. Power is shown as labelled nets, and every GND is common.

Power budget

Six 28BYJ-48 draw about 200 mA each with two coils on: 1.2 A at 100% duty, about 0.7 A at the 60% hold. Add the ESP32 (0.25 A), one BKA30D-R5 motor at a time (0.04 A) and Hall latches (0.02 A): about 1.5 A peak. The 5 V 3 A supply leaves headroom.

Hold switch

The P-channel switch sits on the motors' 5 V, not their ground, so the ULN2803A logic ground stays solid. When it opens, the 1N5819 carries the coil current, so the rail can't swing below ground. The datasheet's 59–118 mN·m friction torque hints the motors may hold unpowered; run the holding-torque test first and leave this out if they do.

Hall timing

The 0x21 MCP23017 raises INTB on any station latch edge (ESP32 GPIO20), and the 0x22 does the same for the year and moon latches (GPIO23); the ESP32 timestamps the interrupt and reads INTCAP to see which latch passed. The machine turns one way, so each station always reports the same edge.

Logic levels

MCP23017s and Hall latches run at 3.3 V; ULN2803A inputs switch fine from 3.3 V. The calendar's coils are driven at 5 V by the 74AHCT245, whose TTL-level inputs take the 0x22 expander's 3.3 V outputs. Step one gauge motor at a time and leave the coils off between moves: that keeps the chip inside its 75 mA supply limit, and the gauge motors hold position unpowered.

USB and the supply

The 1N5819 between the 5 V bus and the ESP32-C6 board's 5V pin keeps a plugged-in USB cable from back-feeding the motor supply, and the supply from back-feeding your computer.

Faceplate

Must be aluminium, never steel: the latches look through it at the magnets. Each station gets a window at 12 o'clock, r 20.5, with two M2 holes for the sensor carrier. The window grows from 3.4 mm square to fit the TO-92 latch (to be re-cut in the Rev M plate).
