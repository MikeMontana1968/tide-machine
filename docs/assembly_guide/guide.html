Tide Machine

A five-constituent harmonic tide predictor: five stepper-driven arms sum on one line and draw the tide on a drum. Assembly, board, smoke test, wiring, first power.

Design and Qualitythe bench of Mike

Datum. Every number is in the machine frame: x along the row (S2 at −224 … M2 at 0, pen +70, drum +155.5), y up, z negative toward you. The faceplate’s front face is z = 0. The figures are drawn from station_study.py, the same model the fit checks run on.

Never turn an arm at constant speed, not even to test it by hand with a drill: the firmware drives each arm at an uneven rate so the line length is exactly sinusoidal.

Known issues found while writing this guide

check before you order or cut
  1. KI 1

    No Rev M cut files yet. tide_machine.rb, faceplate.py and rails.py still describe Rev L. The hole schedule in step 1 comes from station_study.py. Port it to the Ruby before ordering the plate or drilling the rails. The #6 frame-screw holes (Ø4.0 at y ±82) have no spacing yet. Step 1, step 14.

  2. KI 2

    Hall windows are still sized for the SOT-23. They’re 3.4 × 3.4; the TO-92 latch needs about 4.4 × 3.4, and its sensing element sits a few tenths deeper. Re-run the field check (the magnet gap was 4.40 mm) when you resize them. Step 1, step 5.

  3. KI 3

    No holes for the main board yet. Proposed: 4 × Ø3.2 at (25, 39), (−108, 39), (25, −35.28), (−108, −35.28), for the board upright as seen from behind. Checked against the whole model (the standoffs, the board and the lead ends under it): nothing within 3 mm. Screw heads under a dial are fine at 2.4 mm tall or less (the same 1.1 mm as the motor-ear screws). Step 30.

  4. KI 4

    The Hall carriers and the calendar carrier are hand-cut perfboard, not designed parts. As a plain 36 × 68 rectangle, the calendar carrier’s top corners clipped the N2 and K1 motor bodies (8.9 mm³ each; each corner is 11.7 mm from a motor’s axis, against its 14 mm radius). Fixed in the model: cut 4 × 6 off both top corners; it now clears by 0.98 mm. Still open: the two bottom holes at y −78 are under the bottom rail (its inner face is at y −76), so their M2 heads have nowhere to go on the front; and all four nuts sit inside the BKA placeholder’s outline. Move the holes once the motor is measured. Step 5, step 6.

  5. KI 5

    BKA30D-R5 is a placeholder. The body, the shaft diameters, lengths and positions, and whether both shafts turn continuously (the family datasheet shows stops) are all unconfirmed. The year dial’s and moon disc’s hub bores are placeholders, and so is the coil-to-pin map in step 34. Step 6, step 10, step 34.

  6. KI 6

    Arm-bearing axles clamped a washer on the shield. The M3 × 8 went through an M3 washer (Ø7) against the V623ZZ’s face; the inner race is Ø5, so 1 mm of the washer bore on the shield. The pen pulley’s M3 × 16 head (Ø5.7) did the same by 0.35 mm. Fixed in the model: a 3 × 5 × 0.5 shim under each (16 shims in all). The pen pulley’s screw now engages its insert by 2.25 mm. Step 9, step 12.

  7. KI 7

    The pen arm ran through the +x guide rod (27.9 mm³) at the drum’s mid-plane (z −22 to −26, rod at −23.75). Fixed in the model: the arm runs 1 mm in front of the rods, at z −26.25 to −30.25; the nib meets the drum at x 111.2. The pen holder, leaf spring, retract lever and ballast pocket still aren’t designed. Step 12.

  8. KI 8

    Drum drive not designed. Nothing is drawn yet for the motor mount, the coupling to the 7 mm shaft, or how the end caps grip the shaft (the cap STL only has a Ø7.1 bore). The drum motor sits outside the top rail, about 18 cm in a straight line from J10; its lead is about 24 cm. Route it tight or make a 5-way XH extension. Step 15, step 32.

  9. KI 9

    End-post screws are too short. The BOM’s #6 × 3/4″ can’t reach through a 19 mm rail into the post’s end grain. Use #6 × 1-1/2″ (4) with glue; the faceplate still takes #6 × 3/4″ (10). Step 13.

  10. KI 10

    The far dead end has no fixing. Nothing slides at S2’s IN pulley, so the line can end there, but no tie-off point is designed. The pen bracket’s screw-adjustable anchor, copied onto S2’s bracket, would do. Step 16.

  11. KI 11

    Decals use placeholder constituents. Put NOAA G values, the longitude and the epoch into decals.py, and check the K1/O1 ±90° convention (it moves those zeros by 12 h), before printing. Step 8.

  12. KI 12

    No firmware yet. Everything in section E and F tagged planned needs it: the inverse tables, the Hall edge timing, homing, NTP, the PWM hold. Section E.

  13. KI 13

    No 1:1 rail templates yet. rails.py printed drilling templates for Rev L; Rev M needs new ones from the model, printed at 100% and tiled across Letter pages (the rail is 498.9 long), with a calibration bar, page-join marks, and each sheet labelled top or bottom rail, face, and S2 end. Until then, measure from the S2 end. Top rail, inner face: #4 pilots at 50, 106, 162, 218, 274 and 344 (each ± 11), 15 from the rear edge; rod sockets Ø3.1 × 9 at 325.08 and 362.92, 23.75 from the rear edge (the bottom rail’s inner face takes the same two). Both rails: the drum hole at 429.45, 24 from the rear edge. Rear faces: faceplate pilots 6 from the inner edge (spacing with KI 1). Outer faces: end-post screws 9.5 from each end. Step 11, step 13.

Fixed in the model while writing this guide

  • The calendar sat at x −196 in the fit study but −140 in the spec; now −140 everywhere.
  • The left end post ran into S2’s dial, magnets and ear screws; the rails grew 12 mm (now 499; left post 5 mm clear of the dial).
  • The calendar index hand’s base overlapped N2’s dial; it was narrowed and moved down (1.09 mm clear).
  • The index tab and the calendar hand had no pilot holes for their M2 screws; both STLs now have a Ø1.6 pilot.
  • KI 4, KI 6 and KI 7 above. Each fix was confirmed with a real interference check (manifold intersections of every pair of parts, check_interference.py) and the fit study’s 31 clearances, all ≥ 0.98 mm.

2

Glue the magnets into the dials

  • Turn each hub_dial.stl over, back face up. The two 5 × 2 pockets sit at r 20.5, opposite the rim notch.
  • Seen from the back with the notch at 12 o’clock: N face up in the right-hand pocket, S face up in the left. A drop of CA in each pocket.
  • Same on the year dial (pockets at r 20.5) and the moon disc (r 9): seven pairs in all.

The latch switches where the field flips sign between the two magnets, so the edge hardly moves with gap or temperature (0.15° per mT of drift). The same order on every dial makes every station report the same edge.

Check each face with a spare magnet before gluing: one face pulls it, the other pushes it away.

Press in the heat-set inserts

Bolt the motors to the back of the plate

7

Press the dials onto the shafts; fit the index tabs

  • Plate face up. Each hub + dial: the D-bore over the shaft’s flats, and press until the shaft tip is flush with the boss. The dial’s back is then 3.5 mm off the plate.
  • An index tab at 12 o’clock above each dial, one M2 × 6 from behind into its pilot.
  • Turn each dial by hand: it must clear the ear-screw heads, the tab and its neighbours (4.0 mm apart).

The D-bore carries the torque and a light press holds it: the line pulls in the dial’s plane, so nothing pushes it off. For a loose one, a dab of clear silicone. Not threadlocker, which barely cures against plastic.

Thread the line

KI 10
S2N2K1O1M2pen
in overfrontrearfrontrearfrontrear
out overrearfrontrearfrontrearfront = anchor
  • Crimp a loop in the far end of the 0.30 mm fluorocarbon and fix it at S2’s bracket, front plane.
  • At each station: over the IN pulley, down the +x side, round under the arm’s bearing, up the −x side, over the OUT pulley. Then along the rail to the next one.
  • At the pen: over the rear pulley, round under the carriage’s pen pulley, up to the anchor screw on the front plane. Crimp it there.

The order mirrors at every station, so every run between stations stays in one plane and only the legs skew (about 2°). The line passes round each arm bearing and never attaches: the U-turn turns a rise d into 2d of take-up, and the pen pulley halves it again, so the pen moves 35 mm per metre of tide.

C

Smoke test

Nothing expensive goes in until every rail reads right. Use a bench supply at 5.0 V with a 100 mA limit, or a 5 V supply through a USB power meter.

23

Test 0: Look, then measure

  • Look at every joint under a lens: no bridges, no dull balls. Socket notches, diode bands, C1’s stripe, Q1’s tab: all as the silkscreen.
  • Unpowered, meter on ohms: +5V to GND climbs as C1 charges and ends above 1 kΩ. Same from J3’s centre pin to GND.

A reading that stays near 0 Ω is a short: find it before any power goes on.

TIDE-AG
24

Test 1: First power, current-limited

  • Power into J3. LED1 lights and the supply reads about 3 mA: (5.0 − 2.1 V) through R8’s 1 kΩ.

If the supply hits its limit, switch off at once and go back to Test 0.

TIDE-AG
25

Test 2: The 5 V rail

  • 4.95 to 5.00 V: the supply less F1’s drop at 3 mA, which is next to nothing.
TIDE-AG
26

Test 3: The hold switch

  • Power on, nothing plugged in: HOLD reads 0 V. R7 holds Q2 off, so R5 holds Q1 off.
  • Jumper J2 pin 6 (PWM) to J2 pin 1 (ESP_5V): HOLD rises to about 4.99 V. Remove the jumper.
TIDE-AG
27

Test 4: The ESP32’s 5 V

  • 4.8 to 4.95 V: +5V less D1’s Schottky drop at almost no load.

D1 keeps the supply and the C6’s own USB apart, so flashing over USB with the board powered is safe.

TIDE-AG
28

Test 5: Plug in the C6

  • Power off. The C6 into J1/J2, LCD up, USB-C at the board’s left edge. Power on.
  • 3.30 V on J4 pin 1 (from the C6’s regulator). Also 3.3 V on SDA and SCL (J4 pins 3, 4; R1, R2), on INT_A and INT_B (J1 pins 6, 5), and on pin 2 of every Hall socket J20–J26 (RN2).
TIDE-AG
29

Test 6: Chips in

  • Power off. Chips in, notch to notch: U2, U3, U8 MCP23017, U4, U5, U6 ULN2803A, U7 74AHCT245. Check no leg folded under.
  • Power on. Every calendar line on J5 reads 0 V (RN1 holds U7’s inputs low until the 0x22 expander is set up), and HOLD still reads 0.
  • The current is the C6’s own draw plus about 3 mA. Above 250 mA: switch off and look for a chip in backwards.

The I²C scan (0x20, 0x21, 0x22) is step 38: it needs firmware.

39

Holding-torque test

today
  • One spare 28BYJ-48 on the bench, a 100 mm lever on its shaft, a known weight on the end: torque = m · g · 0.1.
  • Unpowered first, then with one coil energised (red to 5 V, orange to GND).

M2’s arm needs 14.5 mN·m. The datasheet’s 59–118 mN·m “friction torque” hints the gearbox holds that unpowered. If so, the PWM hold can go to zero between steps.

Check every motor’s direction

planned

Hall edges

planned

Read the dials

today
