Arm Station Study

Two neighbouring stations, O1 and M2, at the 56 mm pitch: the tightest pair, because M2’s arm sweeps over O1’s dial. Each rotor is a hub with a Ø52 dial and a bolt-on arm carrying a V623ZZ. A Hall latch on a thumbnail PCB behind the faceplate looks through a small window at a pair of magnets in the back of the dial. With nothing tall in front of the plate, the dials sit 3.5 mm off it and the whole rotor stack is 8 mm shallower than with the optical sensors. Above each shaft, two V-groove bearings share one axle; the threading is mirrored at alternate stations. Press Play to watch everything clear everything else.

Threading

Each station's line comes in over one pulley, drops on the +x side, U-turns under the arm's bearing, and climbs on the −x side to the other pulley. Alternate stations swap the order, so every run between stations stays in one plane. Fleet angle is the worst skew of a leg out of its pulley's plane (±2.25 mm over the shortest leg). It peaks only at the top of M2's stroke. Because the arm and rail bearings are the same part, the legs hang exactly vertical at mid-stroke, whatever the real groove radius turns out to be.

Per station

Printed (all without support): hub + dial (dial down, boss up), arm (flat, boss up), index tab, pulley bracket.
Bought: 28BYJ-48 + ULN2003 board, DRV5013 Hall latch on a thumbnail PCB, 2 × 5×2 N52 magnets, 3 × V623ZZ (2 on the rail, on one M3×20 + nyloc with a 3×5×0.5 shim; 1 on the arm, on an M3×8 + washer into an M3×4 heat-set insert), 2 × M3 pan head + nut (motor), 2 × M2 (arm to hub), 3 × M2 (Hall PCB, index tab), 2 × #4 wood screw. No grub screw: the dial is a light press on the flats.

Why two printed rotor parts. When real NOAA constituents replace the placeholders, only the arms get reprinted; the hubs, dials, magnets and sensors stay put. Each part also prints flat without support.

Why Hall sensors, and why a magnet pair. Aluminium is non-magnetic, so the sensor lives behind the plate with its chip nested in a 3.4 mm window, 4.4 mm from the magnets. The two magnets present opposite poles, so the field flips sign sharply between them and the latch switches at that zero crossing: about 0.15° of edge shift per millitesla of threshold drift, well inside the 0.7° a 28BYJ-48 loses when it skips. The faceplate must never be steel.

Index. Each dial is read against a small printed tab at 12 o'clock, just outside its rim; the Hall sensor sits on the same radius line, so the home event happens at a known reading. The calendar has one index hand that crosses the year dial and stops at the moon disc’s rim. The machine only turns one way, so the timing edge is always the same edge.

Built by station_study.py, which also prints these clearances. Sensor dimensions are from Vishay document 81147; motor dimensions from the Kiatronics STEPD-01 datasheet.
